# Assignment 5: Scotty3D Sandbox

The goal of this assigment is to allow you to explore your interests in the field of computer graphics by providing you freedom to implement any feature of your choice into Scotty3D. This will also give you more experience with designing the implementation yourself in an environment that isn't as heavily scaffolded.

This assignment differs from A0 to A4 in timing. A5 is designed to let you extend Scotty3D with a feature based on concepts and techniques related to those covered in A1 and/or A2. The goal for A5.5 is to extend Scotty3D with a feature based on concepts and techniques related to those covered in A3 and/or A4. As such, these assignments will span the entire semester and will be released in parallel with other assignments. Thus it is up to you to determine what topics interest you and plan your own timeline of implementing your feature.

## Hand-in Instructions

To turn in your work, make sure you have all the files in the proper place and login to your GitHub account on the Gradescope submission page. Gradescope will extract submissions from your Scotty3D GitHub repository by automatically pulling your most recent commit, so **it's really important to remember to push all new changes before you submit.**

Details:
- Your feature must work "out of the box". This means that the TAs grading shouldn't need to edit more than a flag at the top of a file to enable the feature/get it to run. 
- Since this assignment is so open-ended, we won't have any test cases to run after you submit. Because of this, a large portion of your grade is your writeup, in which you will communicate what you implemented and how.
- In addition to the code and writeup, you will be submitting a video demo of your feature. This should demonstrate the feature's capabilities. It is up to you to communicate the extent of your feature's capabilities as the **TAs will not be stress testing your feature**. As such, even if you have the most amazing feature, if the video doesn't communicate this to the TAs, it won't get full credit.

## Evaluation
### Write-up
You will submit a document with the details of your implementation. We have provided writeup-A5.0.html and writeup-A5.5.html for you, but feel free to write your report in Latex/Google Docs, etc. instead. Just please make sure that your final writeup and demo video are under the A5-writeup folder.

Your writeup must include the following information:

**Explanation of your feature** - What did you implement? What is it used for? What are its capabilities? What are its limitations?

**Instructions on how to use it** - Where does your feature live? How would a user interact with it? Any command line flags?

**How it was implemented** - What design decisions did you make? Did you handle any special edge cases? How does your code work?

**How it was tested** - How did you test to ensure your feature works? How would someone else recreate these tests? Did you write any unit tests? If so, where?

**Sources used** - What sources did you reference? For what parts of your assignment? Did you use AI? If so, how?

### Demo Video
Your demo video must not be longer than 5 minutes and must show your feature in action. This can range from a edited video with narration to just a simple screen recording of Scotty3D. However, you should demo the full extent of your feature. Think to yourself, "if a TA didn't look at my code or writeup, would they get the full context of what I implemented?" If the answer is "no", then you might want to include more in the video.
* If your video file ends up being too large for GitHub, please do NOT use GitHub LFS, instead **upload your video as a Youtube or Google Drive link** with the correct permissions so that we can view it. This makes it so you do not need a full video file in your Scotty3D push. 

### Code
We will be reading your code and also running your projects ourselves. 

#### Notes:
* If your implementation does not fully function, write an additional short explanation of what your code can and cannot do, and (optionally) briefly explain your strategy and how you got stuck. If your explanation indicates significant understanding of the problem, we may award partial credit even though the code does not work.
* If you substantially rely on AI to help write this assignment, we expect a higher quality of feature implemented. An example would be adding a button to the Scotty3D GUI versus just having a flag at the top of the file.

## A5.0 Project Ideas
You are free to implement whatever feature you want, as long as it is related to the topics covered in A1 or A2. If you're unsure of your idea, feel free to post on piazza or ask a TA/instructor. Here are some examples of potential projects:

### A1
- Low-discrepancy anti-aliasing sampler
- Reimplement your rasterizer in GPU code (OpenGL/Vulkan/DirectX/CUDA, etc)
- Tile-based rasterization
- Deferred rendering
- Support classical texture compression in the pipeline
- Shadow maps
- Screen-space reflections
- Screen-space ambient occlusion/Global Illumination for rasterization
- Implementing Tutte's theorem to manually UV unwrap meshes

### A2
- Mesh Simplification (if you didn't pick it for your global operation)
- Implement 5-gon & 6-gon support
- STL compatibility with scotty3D & show the 3D print
- Loop cut operation 
- Blendshapes/shape keys
- Bridge edge loops operation
- Booleans for geometry (union/difference, etc)
- Per-face textures

Note that these are also fairly reflective of the project scope and difficulty that we expect from you. Even if your project accomplishes everything, but is too small in scope, we will not grant you full credit. Make sure to ask the TAs if you're unsure of your project scope!
